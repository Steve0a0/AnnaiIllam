This pack requests legal advice. It does not classify any worker and must not be treated as a legal opinion.

## Engagement request

Please provide a signed, dated written opinion on the lawful operating model for the Annai Illam staffing platform in India, starting with Tamil Nadu. The opinion must assess the actual operating facts below, not only the labels used in draft agreements or the app.

The opinion must identify whether Annai Illam should operate as one or more of the following, or under another model identified by counsel:

1. Direct employer of workers.
2. Contractor supplying contract labour to a client that is the principal employer.
3. Aggregator, marketplace, or intermediary for independent workers.
4. A category-specific or transaction-specific hybrid of the above.

For every viable model, state the required registrations, agreements, payroll treatment, social-security duties, workplace responsibilities, records, notices, insurance, and launch restrictions. Explain why rejected models do not fit the actual facts.

## Current operating facts to validate with management

The following are facts reflected by the current product and code. Product and Operations must correct any item that does not match intended real-world operations before counsel relies on it.

- Clients submit requirements for worker category, quantity, worksite, dates, shift, and duration.
- Annai Illam administrators approve or reject requirements, create quotes, select workers, assign or replace workers, and can deactivate worker accounts.
- Workers provide identity documents, selfie, skills, experience, availability, and payout details. Annai Illam administrators approve onboarding.
- Workers may accept or decline an assignment, but the platform controls which assignments are offered and records availability and matching.
- The client controls the physical worksite and communicates the required duties and site instructions.
- The platform records GPS-backed check-in/check-out. Administrators verify or correct attendance and may mark absence or lateness.
- The platform calculates worker gross pay from an assignment daily rate and verified attendance, records deductions, calculates net pay, and records payouts.
- The client quote can contain a client rate and worker daily rate. The platform records the resulting margin.
- The platform processes client payments, tax invoices, complaints, disputes, replacements, worker issues, and refunds.
- Current product language uses terms including worker, salary, payroll, deduction, payout, assignment, attendance, approval, and platform margin.
- Current worker terms deliberately state that the app itself does not decide employment status. They must be replaced or amended to match the signed memo.

## Questions the signed memo must answer

### A. Classification and responsibility

1. For each proposed work category, who is the employer, contractor, principal employer, aggregator, or intermediary, and why?
2. Which facts carry the most classification risk: selection, rate setting, attendance, supervision, discipline, exclusivity, substitution, integration, equipment, duration, or economic dependence?
3. Does the answer change for households, companies, factories, construction sites, security work, domestic work, or other worker categories?
4. Can Annai Illam lawfully use more than one model? If so, what controls prevent the models from being mixed in one assignment?
5. Which responsibilities remain with Annai Illam even when the client is principal employer or the worker is not a direct employee?

### B. Applicable law and commencement

Counsel must identify the law actually in force on the memo date and any transition or grandfathering rules. At minimum, consider the current central labour codes and rules, applicable Tamil Nadu rules, wage law, social security, occupational safety, contract labour, shops and establishments, gratuity, bonus, maternity, equal remuneration/non-discrimination, POSH, professional tax, and category-specific licensing.

The starting primary sources include the Ministry of Labour and Employment's current [Labour Codes page](https://www.labour.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/labour-codes-gzNzQzMtQWa), [EPFO employer guidance](https://www.epfindia.gov.in/site_en/For_Employers.php/FAQ.php), the [ESI Act published by ESIC](https://esic.gov.in/Publications/ESIAct1948Amendedupto010610.htm), and the Ministry's [Contract Labour Act and Central Rules](https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/thecontractlabourregulationabolition_act.pdf). These are research prompts, not a substitute for counsel checking current notifications and state rules.

### C. Payroll and social security

1. Define wages, wage period, employer, employee, contribution base, and permitted deductions for the selected model.
2. State EPF, ESI, gratuity, bonus, leave, overtime, minimum wage, professional tax, labour welfare fund, TDS, and any aggregator/social-security duties.
3. For EPF and ESI, identify the responsible establishment, headcount/wage thresholds, direct and contract-worker treatment, enrolment timing, contribution rates, records, returns, and principal-employer exposure.
4. Specify whether daily-rate calculation is lawful for each category and what minimum wage, overtime, weekly rest, holiday, and wage-period controls are required.
5. List every lawful deduction type, evidence/consent requirement, cap, recovery rule, and prohibited deduction.
6. Specify payslip, attendance, muster roll, wage register, bank-payment, nomination, UAN/IP, and retention requirements.
7. Explain treatment of no-shows, cancelled assignments, replacements, client non-payment, advances, refunds, and worker complaints without unlawfully withholding wages.

### D. Agreements and product controls

1. Provide approved client and worker agreement clauses, including party roles and responsibility allocation.
2. Identify product terms that must change, including salary/payroll labels if inappropriate.
3. State which terms require separate affirmative consent or a signed engagement letter before an assignment.
4. Provide a responsibility matrix for Annai Illam, client, and worker covering safety, supervision, wages, contributions, equipment, misconduct, injury, insurance, grievance, and termination.
5. Identify facts the product must capture before it can determine a lawful rate, assignment, contribution, deduction, or payout.
6. State what must appear in worker-facing rate/pay statements and client-facing orders/invoices.

### E. Launch and remediation

1. List registrations, licences, policies, insurance, agreements, and system changes required before the first controlled pilot.
2. Identify any category, client type, state, or worksite that must be excluded from the pilot.
3. Assess historical exposure and recommend a privileged remediation plan if prior activity used the wrong model.
4. Provide a monitoring trigger for headcount, wage threshold, state expansion, new category, or operational change that requires fresh advice.

## Required memo format and acceptance

The deliverable is accepted only when it:

- Is on counsel letterhead, signed, dated, and identifies the instructed legal entity.
- States the facts and documents reviewed, assumptions, jurisdictions, law/notifications checked, and effective date.
- Selects the operating model for every pilot worker category and explains the classification analysis.
- Includes a compliance and registration matrix with responsible party, threshold, due date/frequency, record, and consequence.
- Includes payroll instructions detailed enough to become engineering acceptance criteria.
- Includes a responsibility matrix and approved client/worker agreement language.
- Lists red flags, open factual questions, prohibited launch scenarios, and a review/expiry trigger.
- Is approved in writing by Product Owner and Executive Sponsor, with Finance acknowledging the payroll implications.
